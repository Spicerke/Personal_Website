Comparing graph convolutional networks for mobility-aid recognition

The dataset was 270 stock videos of people using mobility aids, and none of it was model-ready. I built the extraction pipeline: pull each video from a GCS bucket, run MediaPipe pose estimation for 17 body keypoints, window the sequences at 10s / 5s / 1s, then label and split into train and test as .npy files.

Getting that data to load in the lab's existing GCN codebase — and in Colab, to keep compute cheap — took real surgery on both. Once it ran, I benchmarked three GCN architectures on accuracy and loss. The comparison is unpublished.

Gait analysis

A skeletal GCN is heavy for what we needed, so I asked whether hand-derived gait parameters could get close. After a literature review I extracted 16 features from the same pose data and trained six model families on them to classify mobility-aid type.

That classifier is now running against real footage the lab collected of pedestrians crossing near campus. The goal is auto-labeling: find mobility-aid users in the wild, extract their pose data, and use it to train the next round of models.
